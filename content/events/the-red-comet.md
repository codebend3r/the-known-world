In the wake of the old year a comet took the sky, red as blood and wound, its tail spanning half the heavens, bright enough to be seen by day. No two watchers agreed on its meaning. In King's Landing the Lannisters called it Joffrey's crimson banner; in the North men named it the Red Sword and said it was Ned Stark's blood, crying vengeance; Aeron Damphair saw the flame of the Drowned God, Edmure Tully's rivermen the promise of victory, and old Osha the wildling said only that it meant one thing, boy: dragons. Priests of R'hllor proclaimed the bleeding star of prophecy that heralds Azor Ahai reborn, and Stannis Baratheon drew a burning sword beneath it. Across the world in Qarth, the warlocks marked that it had first blazed over the Dothraki sea on the night a widowed girl walked into her husband's pyre.
