The nine hundred and ninety-eighth Lord Commander had stretched his vows past what many brothers could bear: wildlings through the gates and garrisoned in Watch castles, a wildling princess wed to a king's man at his urging, loans from the Iron Bank, and rangers' heads returned as wights' work piling dread upon resentment. Then came the letter in pink wax from the Bastard of Bolton, taunting and demanding, and Jon Snow announced in the Shieldhall what no Lord Commander could say and remain one: that he would ride south against Ramsay himself, and let any free folk who wished come with him. In the snows that night, by the corpse of the giant Wun Wun, his own officers closed around him — for the Watch, said Bowen Marsh, weeping as he drove the blade. Daggers took the Lord Commander in throat and belly, and he went down into the cold with a single word on his lips: Ghost.
