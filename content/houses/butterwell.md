House Butterwell rose from riverlands dairy money, its critics never letting the family forget its roots as, in their telling, little better than upjumped cattle thieves, though its cellars were said to hold the finest wine outside the Arbor. Fortune smiled after King Maegor I Targaryen exterminated House Harroway in 44 AC and granted Lord Alton Butterwell, his own master of coin, the fallen house's town; Alton later offered his stout, thrice-widowed sister as a royal bride, and though Maegor scorned the match, it was said to have lodged in the king's mind when he chose fertile wives to secure an heir.

The family's fortunes crested under a later Lord Butterwell, who wavered between the blacks and greens during the Dance of the Dragons before choosing fealty to Aegon II over the headsman's axe, and who rose still further as Hand of the King to Aegon IV Targaryen. In payment for a night's hospitality, and by the bawdier tellings for rather more than that with his three unwed daughters, King Aegon gifted his Hand a dragon egg; Lord Butterwell used his new wealth to raise Whitewalls, a castle of pale Vale stone and marble veined with gold, its rafters hewn from weirwood, which the smallfolk nearby took to calling the Milkhouse.

His grandson, Lord Ambrose Butterwell, climbed even higher, serving as master of coin to Aegon IV and Hand of the King to Daeron II, though his halting response to the First Blackfyre Rebellion drew whispers that he favored Daemon Blackfyre in secret; his own sons split between the two claimants and died together on the Redgrass Field, a third lost besides to the Great Spring Sickness. Ambrose's attempt to marry a Frey bride at Whitewalls in 212 AC instead furnished the setting for the Second Blackfyre Rebellion, and Lord Brynden Rivers punished the house by stripping it of all but a tenth of its wealth and dismantling Whitewalls stone by stone, denying the Blackfyre cause a monument. House Butterwell has held no seat since.
