House Farwynd is a scattered ironborn line sworn to the Greyjoys of Pyke, its branches strewn across the westernmost shores of Great Wyk and the lonely isles beyond — among them the Farwynds of Sealskin Point, the Farwynds of the Foul, and, senior and strangest of all, the Farwynds of the Lonely Light. That last seat is a cluster of thirteen wind-gnawed islets eight days' sail northwest of Great Wyk, the westernmost inhabited place in all Westeros, where mariners blown off their course raised a tower and a beacon a thousand years gone and took the name Farwynd thereafter. Their arms show a black longship against a setting sun of dark red and orange, above a black sea — a fitting device for men who dwell at the very edge of the known world, with naught but the Sunset Sea before them.

Mainland ironborn speak of the folk of the Lonely Light with no little unease, for it is said they are skinchangers who slip their skins to swim as seals, sea lions, walruses, and spotted whales, and that the women of that house lie with seals to bear half-human children. Whatever truth lies in such tales, the line is reckoned mad more often than monstrous. At the kingsmoot called on Old Wyk after the death of King Balon Greyjoy — a gathering chronicled in _A Feast for Crows_ — Lord Gylbert Farwynd, a tall and spare man with a lantern jaw and eyes that shifted grey to blue, set forth his own dreaming claim. With his three sons for champions and gifts of sealskins, walrus tusks, and bronze-banded warhorns, he vowed to lead the ironborn west across the Sunset Sea to a land of peace and plenty where "every man shall be a king and every wife a queen." The captains laughed his vision down and crowned Euron Crow's Eye instead, and Gylbert returned to his far beacon with not a single voice raised in his favor.
