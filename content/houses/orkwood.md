House Orkwood of Orkmont is an ironborn house of great antiquity, seated upon the wooded isle whose dark pines they took for their arms. In the long centuries of the kingsmoot, before ever the Andals crossed the narrow sea, the captains and kings of the ironborn raised up Orkwoods among their number more than once, and the reavers of Orkmont were reckoned a power upon the salt waters. Archmaester Haereg counts them among the great reaving houses of the Age of Heroes, when Orkmont's forests gave timber for a thousand longships and the Orkwood kings carried fire and iron to far shores. When the hereditary crown passed to the Greyirons and then the custom of choosing kings was put down, the Orkwoods endured as one of the oldest and proudest of the salt-and-rock kindreds. It was in the days of King Rognar II Greyiron that they wrote their boldest page: when Andal warlords came reaving among the isles, the Orkwoods joined with Houses Hoare, Greyjoy, and Drumm to bring the last of the black-crowned Greyiron line to ruin, wedding Andal queens to share in the spoils, and lifting the Hoares of Orkmont to the driftwood crown in Rognar's place.

In the present day the house is much diminished from that ancient eminence. They are no longer king-makers, but a lordly line of Orkmont sworn to the kraken of Pyke, their old glories the stuff of singers and the dusty pages of _The History of the Ironborn_. Yet the Orkwoods have not forgotten the taste of crowns. When Aeron Damphair called the captains and kings to Old Wyk to choose a successor to Balon Greyjoy, the banners of Orkwood were among those that flew above Nagga's Bones, and Lord Alyn Orkwood (called, after the manner of the isles, simply Orkwood of Orkmont) threw the strength of his twenty longships behind Euron Crow's Eye. He sat at Euron's elbow through the feasting and whispered in his ear, and when the dragon horn was sounded it was Alyn who raised up the man who blew it, that all the ironborn might see. So the kings of old Orkmont, who once made and unmade the rulers of the isles, lent their last weight to the crowning of another.
