House Serrett of Silverhill traces its origins to the coming of the Andals to the westerlands, when an Andal warlord wed into the dwindling line of House Silverstone, the ancient First Men lords who had ruled the silver-rich hills south of Deep Den since the Age of Heroes. The Silverstones had long styled themselves the Pale Kings of that secluded valley, but their line had faded to a single heir, Cerenna Silverstone, and it was through that union that the Serretts rose to claim both the seat and the mines beneath it. The new house adopted the peacock as its sigil and declared their words "I Have No Rival," a boast that spoke to the wealth their silver workings commanded.

Silverhill's value lay underground as much as above. The mines carved into the hills beneath the keep rival even Casterly Rock in the weight of silver they yield, and the Serretts parlayed that wealth into a firm place among the principal bannermen of House Lannister. The house paid a heavy toll during Aegon's Conquest when Lord Gerold Serrett rode against the Targaryen host at the Field of Fire and did not return, his peacock shield said to have been twisted and blackened by dragonflame. Despite such losses the house endured, its silver revenues ensuring that Silverhill remained prosperous through the centuries of Targaryen rule.

At the outset of _A Game of Thrones_, Lord Serrett answers the call of Tywin Lannister and rides with the main Lannister host into the riverlands. He fights alongside Ser Kevan Lannister at the center of the army during the Battle on the Green Fork, standing among the most trusted of the western lords. When Tyrion Lannister is later named acting Hand of the King, he names Lord Serrett among those who might have served as well, a quiet acknowledgment of the house's standing among the great bannermen of the west.
