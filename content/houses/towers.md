House Towers of Harrenhal held the cursed castle for three short generations during the reign of the early Targaryen kings, a riverlands house raised up out of obscurity by the tourney sword of its founder. Ser Walton Towers was the victor of the great tourney that King Maegor I called for the disposition of Harrenhal, after the Harroways were put to the sword. Sorely wounded in the final tilt, Walton was nevertheless named Lord of Harrenhal upon the field; the wounds took him within a fortnight, and the lordship passed to his son Jordan before he ever sat in the Hall of a Hundred Hearths. Five black towers upon a white field, ringed in a double tressure of red and black, was the device the new lords flew above the half-burned curtain walls.

Lord Jordan stood by King Maegor through the dark years of his reign, and held Harrenhal for him until the king's death sent him into King Jaehaerys's peace; the broken holdings of his lordship he could not entirely restore, for the surrounding lands had been parceled out to Butterwell, Darry, and others as Maegor saw fit. Jordan's son Maegor — named for the king his father had served — was the third and last of the line. Sickly all his days, he left no living heir behind him; with his death in 73 AC the line of the towers ended, and King Jaehaerys granted the great castle on to House Strong, in whose hands the curse of Harren the Black would work itself out yet again.
