The Freefolk are not a noble house but the scattered, kinless people of the lands beyond the Wall, descendants of those First Men who chose the haunted forest and the frozen shore over the realms that would one day belong to lords and kings. The maesters and lords of the Seven Kingdoms call them wildlings; they call themselves the free folk, and that name is the whole of their politics. To be free, in their telling, is to bend the knee to no man, and to bend the knee, in their telling, is the chief and surest mark of a fool.

Their country is the cold half of the world, a great arc north of the Wall and west of the Shivering Sea: the Haunted Forest pressed close beneath the ice, the Frostfangs and the valley of the Milkwater, the Frozen Shore where wood is so scarce that men ride bone chariots drawn by dogs, and beyond all that the Lands of Always Winter where no living man goes willingly. Within that country they live as villagers and hunters, raiders and salt-hunters, ice river clans who eat their dead, mountain Hornfoots who run barefoot through the snow, cave-dwelling Nightrunners, and the strange shaved warriors of Thenn who alone among the free folk keep something like a lord. Among them too dwell the last of the giants, who ride mammoths into battle, and the skinchangers who slip from their own skins into wolf and eagle and bear.

The free folk keep the old gods of the weirwoods, as their First Men forebears did, and keep no maester, no septon, and no surname. A woman is not asked for in marriage but stolen, with horn and song and a knife at hand, and a man who cannot steal his bride is reckoned no man worth having. Yet for all their feuds, from age to age a fighter or a sorcerer has stitched the warring bands together for one cause or another and named himself King-Beyond-the-Wall. So came Joramun in the Long Night, who blew the Horn of Winter to wake giants from the earth and joined his strength to King Brandon Stark's to put down the Night's King at the Nightfort. So came the brothers Gendel and Gorne, who led their host south through the caves beneath the Frostfangs and broke upon the spears of the kings of Winter. So came Bael the Bard, who walked into Winterfell with a harp on his back and walked out with a Stark maid for a bride. So came Raymun Redbeard in the days of Lord Willam Stark, his head taken at last on the shore of Long Lake by Harmond Umber, called the Drunken Giant of Last Hearth.

By the events of _A Game of Thrones_ they are gathered again, this time under Mance Rayder, a Night's Watch deserter turned king. He has called every band south of the Frostfangs to come down out of the haunted forest, not for plunder, but to flee south of the Wall, for the cold things long thought dead are stirring once more in the long night beyond the trees. His host marches on the Wall with the Magnar Styr of Thenn at his vanguard, Tormund Giantsbane at his right hand, the spearwife Ygritte and the warg Varamyr Sixskins in his company, and Mag the Mighty at the head of the giants who follow him. When the host breaks against Castle Black and Stannis Baratheon rides up from the south at the gallop, it is to the bastard Lord Commander of the Watch, Jon Snow, that what remains of the free folk at last bends the knee, and through the gate of the Wall that they at last come.
