A slender, handsome man of Lorath with long straight hair that falls white on one side and red on the other, Jaqen H'ghar claims to have been a sellsword of the Free City of Lorath before he was taken from the black cells beneath King's Landing as a criminal. Yoren of the Night's Watch keeps him chained in a wagon with the savage Rorge and the biting madman Biter as he leads his recruits north; though he shares their cage, Jaqen is polite, calm, and forever smiling, and he speaks in the Lorathi manner, naming himself only as "a man" and Arya Stark, who travels disguised as the boy "Arry", as "a boy". When Ser Amory Lorch's men burn the holdfast at the Gods Eye, Arya frees the three prisoners from the flames, and Jaqen tells her that because she stole three deaths from the Red God by saving them, a man now owes her three lives in their place.

At Harrenhal, where the prisoners are pressed into Lord Tywin Lannister's service, Arya names her three deaths. She gives him the torturer Chiswyck, who is soon found with a broken neck beneath a wallwalk, and then the cruel understeward Weese, whose own spotted dog tears out his throat. For the third she tricks him, whispering "Jaqen H'ghar" itself, and to buy back his own name he agrees to help her free the captive northmen and rivermen in the dungeons, cutting down Ser Amory's guards so that the Brave Companions may take the castle for Roose Bolton.

His debt paid, Jaqen bids Arya farewell and changes his face before her eyes, his red-and-white hair becoming a cap of tight black curls. He presses upon her a worn iron coin and the words _valar morghulis_, telling her that should she ever wish to find him again she must give the coin to any man of Braavos and speak those words, then he crosses the narrow sea to his own duties. A man of his new appearance later surfaces at the Citadel of Oldtown, passing himself off as an alchemist and taking the identity of the murdered novice Pate, for Jaqen H'ghar is a Faceless Man, and the Faceless Men keep no single name or face for long.
